The Roman aqueducts were a system of channels built to carry fresh water into cities and towns. They relied entirely on gravity, maintaining a gentle downward slope over very long distances. Where valleys had to be crossed, engineers built towering stone arches to keep the water channel at the right height. The water fed public fountains, bath houses, and some private homes, and was distributed using bronze fittings. The city of Rome alone was served by eleven aqueducts by the third century.
