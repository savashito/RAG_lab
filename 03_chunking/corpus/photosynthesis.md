Photosynthesis is the process by which green plants convert light energy into chemical energy. Inside the chloroplasts, the pigment chlorophyll absorbs sunlight, mostly in the blue and red wavelengths. The plant takes in carbon dioxide through tiny pores called stomata and draws water up from its roots. Using that captured light energy, it combines these ingredients into glucose, a simple sugar that stores energy for later use. As a byproduct of splitting water molecules, the plant releases oxygen into the air. This released oxygen is the source of almost all the oxygen in Earth's atmosphere today.
